The Four Pramāṇas

How the Nyāya School Tests Knowledge

Nyāya, one of the six classical schools of Indian philosophy, holds that a belief only counts as genuine knowledge (pramā) when it is reached through a reliable means of knowing (pramāṇa). Every statement in this validator is checked against all four.

01

Pratyakṣa

Direct Observation

Knowledge gained through direct contact between a sense organ and its object — seeing, hearing, touching, tasting, or smelling something yourself. Nyāya treats this as the most immediate and foundational pramāṇa, since it requires no intermediate reasoning.

e.g. Knowing fire is hot because you felt its warmth.
02

Anumāna

Inference

Knowledge derived by reasoning from something already known to something not directly perceived, based on an invariable relationship (vyāpti) between the two. Classic Nyāya logic runs through a five-step syllogism to reach a valid conclusion.

e.g. Inferring fire on a hill from seeing smoke rising above it.
03

Upamāna

Comparison & Analogy

Knowledge of a new object gained by understanding its similarity to something already familiar. It explains how a description or analogy lets someone recognize a thing correctly the first time they encounter it.

e.g. Recognizing a wild ox after being told it "looks like a cow."
04

Śabda

Authoritative Testimony

Knowledge received from the trustworthy word of a reliable source — a competent expert, a credible witness, or a recognized authoritative text. Validity here rests entirely on the reliability of the speaker or source, not on independent verification.

e.g. Accepting a scientific fact stated in a peer-reviewed textbook.
